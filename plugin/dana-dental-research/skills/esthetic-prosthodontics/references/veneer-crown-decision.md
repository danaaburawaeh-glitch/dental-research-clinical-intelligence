# Veneer vs Crown vs Neither — Decision Rules

Loaded by: esthetic-prosthodontics, clinical-case, treatment-plan-audit.

Same **SOURCE NOTE** as `prosthodontic-restorability.md`: Clinical Protocol **v1.3 is
APPROVED**; no textbook content; no invented thresholds.

## 1. The question that comes first

> **Can the clinical objective be achieved with a more conservative option?**

PROMPT MASTER §8 "Crown Avoidance Check" (operator-authored, **JUDG**). It is asked before a crown
is proposed, and the answer is recorded. "The patient asked for it" is a patient factor, never an
indication (M1 ESTHETIC §2).

## 2. The least-invasive ladder (CORE §7 tiers)

Work **down** this ladder and stop at the first option that meets the biological, functional,
structural and esthetic requirements. Each step down must be justified by something the step above
cannot achieve.

| | Option | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No treatment** — always a live option (CORE §2/§8) | T0 |
| 2 | **Whitening**, where the complaint is colour and the teeth are sound | T0 |
| 3 | **Orthodontics**, where the complaint is position, alignment, spacing or proportion | T0 |
| 4 | **Additive composite** — bonded, repairable, reversible in practice | T1 |
| 5 | **No-prep / additive ceramic**, where enamel volume and space allow | T1 |
| 6 | **Minimal-prep restoration** — minimal-prep veneer, conservative onlay, partial coverage | T2 |
| 7 | **More invasive coverage — only with an independent indication** | T3+ |

**A full crown is never reached by esthetic convenience.** It is reached only when step 7 carries
one of the structural indications in `prosthodontic-restorability.md` R-4.

**Clinic Protocol §7.2 states the prohibition directly (JUDG):** *"aggressive preparation of sound
teeth without a clear indication"* and *"offering veneers as a solution to a problem that needs
orthodontic, periodontal or fundamental functional treatment"* are listed among treatments never
performed. That list is described in the protocol as its ethical backbone.

## 3. When a veneer is NOT the answer

- **Extensive structural compromise.** A veneer restores a facial surface; it does not replace a
  missing wall, protect an undermined cusp, or rebuild a fractured tooth. Structural loss beyond
  what an adhesive facial restoration can support moves the decision to partial or full coverage —
  it does not make the veneer thinner.
- **The complaint is positional.** Spacing, rotation, crowding, proportion driven by malposition —
  the honest answer is orthodontics, or an explicit compromise the patient has understood.
  Presenting veneers as an alternative to orthodontics is on the protocol's prohibited list.
- **Unresolved biology or function.** See §4.
- **Insufficient enamel for bonding.** Adhesive performance depends on the substrate; a veneer
  bonded largely to dentin is a different restoration with a different prognosis, and the material
  and cement decision changes with it (Clinic Protocol §4.2, **JUDG + IFU**).
- **Isolation cannot be achieved.** Clinic Protocol §4.1 — postpone rather than accept an
  unpredictable adhesive protocol (**JUDG**).

## 4. Biology and function precede esthetics — non-negotiable

CORE §2: *biology before esthetics; function before irreversible restorative intervention where
function materially affects prognosis.*

**Clinic Protocol §7.1 (L1, anchored to R1 — Sanz et al. 2020, EFP S3 guideline,
doi:10.1111/jcpe.13290):** veneers and definitive restorations are **not started** in the presence
of active gingival inflammation, uncontrolled bleeding, high plaque or calculus, untreated
periodontal pockets, gingival overgrowth or instability, or an unperformed need for crown
lengthening or tissue modification. No final impression or scan is taken before the tissues are
healthy enough to record margins accurately, and no esthetic plan is completed before the gingival
level has stabilised post-surgically for the period the periodontist specifies.

**Parafunction is assessed before extensive ceramic work.** Clinic Protocol §7.2 lists *"ignoring
occlusion and parafunction in extensive ceramic cases"* as never acceptable (**JUDG**), and §5.1
anchors the assessment elements to the 2025 international bruxism consensus (**L4**, R4 — Verhoeff
et al., doi:10.1111/joor.13985), which grades confidence as reported / clinical / instrumentally
confirmed. Use that grading rather than a bare "bruxer / not a bruxer".

## 5. Material selection follows the case, not the colour

Clinic Protocol §2.1 (**JUDG**): the choice rests on remaining enamel, substrate shade, restoration
thickness, occlusion, masking requirement and margin location — **not on shade alone**. Zirconia
in the anterior is for selected cases where strength or substrate masking is the priority; it is
**not automatically the first choice** (**IFU + L2**, R7 — Ma et al., doi:10.1016/j.jdent.2026.106974;
the protocol itself flags that this reference's publication status should be re-checked when
cited). Feldspathic and lithium disilicate laminate veneer outcomes are supported by **L2** (R5,
doi:10.11607/ijp.4315).

Cementation is governed by **(IFU)** throughout (Clinic Protocol §4). Two material rules there are
absolute, not preferences: hydrofluoric acid is never used on zirconia, and silane alone is not
relied upon for zirconia — an MDP-containing primer is required.

## 6. Expectation gate for elective esthetic requests (M1 ESTHETIC)

Before any T2+ elective esthetic intervention: expectation calibration in the patient's own words ·
proportionality of the request to the finding · history of repeated dissatisfaction elsewhere ·
preoccupation with a slight or non-existent flaw → note the possibility of body dysmorphic disorder
as a **screening prompt for the clinician**, never a diagnosis of the patient · reversible test
phase (wax-up and intraoral mock-up, approval documented) is **mandatory** · phonetic and functional
verification for anterior and OVD changes · consent elements quantifying tissue removal.

Clinic Protocol §7.2 also prohibits (**JUDG**) copying another person's smile literally without
regard to the patient's face and function, and executing a shade or form seen as professionally
unacceptable *even at the patient's insistence*.

## QC check

The conservative alternative was considered and the answer recorded. A crown carries an independent
structural indication. Biology and function were cleared before esthetic planning. A reversible
test phase precedes any elective irreversible esthetic work. Absence is a QC FAIL.
